Mar Mikhael is not like other nightlife districts. It's one street, packed end to end with small bars, each with its own sound system, its own regulars, and its own idea of what the night should feel like. People don't settle into one room for the night — they move. A group might be in your bar for fifteen minutes before they're out the door and into the next one.

We sat down with Tony Schwery, who's played rooms up and down the strip over the years, to talk about what that actually demands from a DJ — and why it's a completely different skill set from a wedding or a resident club night.

## Why Is Mar Mikhael Different From a Regular Club Set?

**"In a club, you're building one long arc for one crowd that's there for hours. In Mar Mikhael, you're building dozens of tiny arcs, back to back, for a crowd that keeps replacing itself."**

Tony explains it like this: a normal set has an opening, a rise, a peak, a cooldown. On the strip, you might get through your "opening" three times in an hour because a new wave of people just walked in and the ones who arrived twenty minutes ago are already heading to the next bar.

"You have to make every fifteen-minute window feel complete on its own. You can't wait forty minutes to build to your best record. If it's a good night, that record needs to land in the first ten minutes, because that's how long some of these people are staying."

## How Do You Read a Room That's Constantly Changing?

This is where 25 years behind the decks pays off. Tony says he's not really reading one room anymore — he's reading the street.

* **Watch who's walking past the window.** If a group slows down and looks in, that's your next crowd forming before they've even ordered a drink.
* **Track the volume of the room, not just the size.** A half-full bar that's loud and talking is closer to dancing than a packed room that's quiet.
* **Use short records on purpose.** Edits and radio-length versions do more work here than a nine-minute extended mix. You need the hook, not the journey.
* **Keep a "grab" record ready at all times.** Something instantly recognisable — 90s, disco, a well-known Arabic crossover — that can turn a room in under sixty seconds.

"On a strip like Mar Mikhael, hesitation costs you the crowd. You don't have the luxury of easing people in."

## Does Genre Matter as Much in a Bar as a Club?

Less than people think, Tony says — but the transitions matter more.

Because the bars sit next to each other and people bounce between them, there's an unspoken competition happening in real time. If the bar next door is playing something with more energy, you'll feel your room lean toward the door. Tony's approach is to stay flexible across decades rather than lock into one lane.

"I'll go from a 90s house classic into something Arabic into a 2010s crossover, back to back. Genre purity is a club-night idea. On this street, **your job is momentum, not curation.**"

He's quick to add that this doesn't mean playing everything at once with no logic — it means the BPM and energy have to stay connected even when the genres jump. Camelot wheel thinking still applies; it's just compressed into a much smaller window.

## What Gear Actually Works in a Small Bar Setup?

Space is tight on the Mar Mikhael strip, and Tony says the gear conversation is more practical here than anywhere else he plays.

* **Compact Pioneer CDJ setups** are still the standard — reliable, familiar to any venue's in-house system, and quick to patch in.
* **Rekordbox-prepped USBs** save real time when you're moving between venues or covering for another DJ mid-week — no laptop negotiation, just plug and go.
* **A tight, well-tagged library beats a huge one.** When you've got ninety seconds to find the right transition, you need your crates organized by energy and BPM, not just by genre. ## What Does This Mean for Someone Booking a DJ for a Bar or Private Party in Beirut?

If you're a venue owner or event planner on the Mar Mikhael strip — or planning a private party that has that same fast, social energy — Tony's advice is simple: **hire someone who's comfortable being interrupted.**

"A resident DJ in a big club gets to plan a journey. A DJ on this street has to be ready to throw the plan out every fifteen minutes and still make it feel intentional. That only comes from doing it for years, not from a good playlist."

That adaptability is exactly what he brings to any booking — a wedding reception in Faqra, a private party in Beirut, a corporate event in Doha, or a night on the Mar Mikhael strip itself.
