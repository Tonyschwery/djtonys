Every couple planning a wedding in Beirut or Faqra eventually asks me some version of the same question: "Do we need permission to play this song?" It usually comes up after they've built a dream playlist and someone mentions copyright. It's a fair question, and the honest answer is more nuanced than most people expect.

I've been playing clubs, weddings, and private events across Lebanon and the Gulf for more than 25 years. Licensing rarely makes headlines the way a great set does, but it shapes how a professional DJ actually operates behind the scenes. Here's how it really works.

## Who actually needs a music license?

The short version: **the venue or event organizer usually carries the licensing responsibility, not the individual guest hosting a private party.**

In most countries, including Lebanon and the Gulf states, public venues — clubs, hotels, restaurants, event halls — are expected to hold a blanket license with a local collecting society or performing rights organization. That license covers the music played on their premises, whether it's a resident DJ's Friday night set or a wedding reception they're hosting that weekend. It's built into how the venue operates, the same way they handle a liquor license or a fire safety permit.

A private wedding at a family home or a rented villa in Faqra sits in a slightly different category. It's not generally treated as a public performance in the same way a nightclub night is, which is why most couples never think about it. But the moment that private event becomes public — livestreamed, posted in full to YouTube, or broadcast to a diaspora audience watching from abroad — the rules shift.

## Why livestreaming changes the equation

This is the part that catches people off guard. I've written before about how [Beirut and Faqra weddings now reach diaspora screens worldwide](#) through livestreams, and that's become standard practice for Lebanese families with relatives scattered across the Gulf, Europe, and North America.

The problem: platforms like YouTube and Instagram run automated content recognition on uploaded audio. A four-hour livestream of a wedding reception, with a DJ mixing commercial tracks for most of the night, can trigger copyright claims, muted audio, or even a takedown — regardless of whether the event itself was private.

**If you're planning to livestream your reception, tell your DJ in advance.** A good DJ can plan around it:

- Using royalty-free or cleared intros and transitions during the stream window
- Timing the stream to cover the ceremony and key moments rather than the entire night
- Working with a streaming provider that has its own licensing arrangements with platforms
- Keeping a separate, unrestricted set for the in-room sound system

This is a logistics conversation, not a creative one, but it matters just as much as choosing the first dance song.

## What about corporate events?

Corporate events in Beirut, Doha, and Dubai sit closer to the venue's responsibility, but with one added layer: brand activations and sponsored events sometimes need explicit sync licenses if music is paired with recorded video content that the company plans to reuse in marketing. That's a different right entirely from simply playing a track over a PA system for guests, and it's worth raising early with whoever is producing the event's recap video.

If your event includes professional filming for post-event promotion, loop your DJ and your videographer into the same conversation before the day. It's far easier to pick music with reuse rights in mind than to re-edit a beautiful recap video months later because a track can't be cleared.

It's partly about audio quality, since a clean WAV or AIFF file holds up far better on a big club system than a compressed stream rip. ## Practical questions to ask before you book

If you're planning a wedding, private party, or corporate event in Beirut, Faqra, Doha, or Dubai, these are worth asking upfront:

1. **Is the venue's licensing current?** Most established hotels and halls handle this automatically, but it's worth a quick confirmation, especially for newer or outdoor venues.
2. **Are you livestreaming the event, in full or in part?** If yes, flag it with your DJ and streaming provider separately.
3. **Will there be professional video content used for marketing afterward?** That's a different right than playing music live for guests.
4. **Does your DJ source music professionally?** A DJ working from a legitimate, well-maintained library is also less likely to run into playback or quality issues on the night.
