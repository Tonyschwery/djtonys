October feels too early to think about Christmas. It isn't. If you run a venue in Mar Mikhael, plan corporate parties in Beirut, or own a chalet in Faqra that fills up every winter weekend, you already know the real calendar: **December books out by mid-autumn**, not by December itself.

Bar owners in Mar Mikhael start confirming their DJ lineup for the whole month before Halloween even happens. By the time most people start "thinking about" their Christmas party, the good dates and the DJs they actually want are gone.

## Why December fills up faster than any other month in Lebanon

Lebanon's nightlife and event calendar compresses an entire year of celebrations into about five weeks. Between the first week of December and Orthodox New Year in January, you've got:

- **Corporate year-end parties** — companies closing the fiscal year and rewarding staff
- **Private Christmas gatherings** — family reunions, especially with diaspora returning for the holidays
- **Engagement parties and weddings** — December is one of Lebanon's busiest wedding windows, right alongside summer
- **Club takeovers in Mar Mikhael and Gemmayze** — themed nights running almost every weekend
- **New Year's Eve itself** — the single most requested date of the entire year

Every one of those events wants the same thing: a weekend night, a known DJ, and enough notice to actually plan the run of the evening. That demand doesn't spread evenly across December. It stacks on three or four Fridays and Saturdays, plus the 24th, 25th, and 31st. There simply aren't enough prime dates to go around.

## What this means if you're planning a Faqra weekend or a Beirut party

Faqra in December is a different animal from Faqra in July. The crowd skips between chalet house parties, après-ski lounges, and one or two bigger private events that become the talk of the season. If you want your night to be the one people remember, you need a DJ who already knows how a mountain crowd behaves after dark — denser energy curve, shorter warm-up, because everyone arrives ready to move, not easing in over two hours like a summer rooftop.

In Beirut and Mar Mikhael, the dynamic is slightly different. Bars and lounges are running back-to-back bookings for the entire month, and a lot of hosts assume they can sort the music out "closer to the date." The DJs worth hiring don't work that way in December — their weekends are the first thing to go.

## What to lock in during October, not December

If you're hosting anything between the first week of December and New Year's, here's what actually needs deciding now:

1. **The date and the backup date.** Prime Fridays and Saturdays go first. If your first choice isn't available, you want options before everyone else is scrambling too.
2. **The DJ, confirmed in writing.** A verbal "sounds good" in October becomes a problem in December when three other hosts want the same name for the same night.
3. **The vibe brief.** Corporate crowd versus family reunion versus club night each need a completely different set — tell your DJ which one you're running so the music is built for the room, not improvised on the spot.
4. **Sound and lighting logistics**, especially for Faqra chalets and outdoor terraces where power and acoustics aren't as straightforward as a built-out venue.
5. **Language mix.** Lebanese December crowds often include returning diaspora family — Arabic, French, and English all need a seat at the table, and that's worth discussing before the night, not during it.

## Building the actual holiday set

A great December set in Lebanon isn't just a Christmas playlist with a dance floor bolted on. It's a careful blend: festive touches early in the night when families and older relatives are still around, then a shift into the Arabic, 80s, 90s, and current house tracks that actually fill a Beirut or Faqra dance floor once the night opens up. The transition point — when the room goes from polite to loose — is the part that takes 25 years to get right, and it changes depending on whether you're in a chalet living room or a Mar Mikhael bar.

## What hosts get wrong about booking early

The most common mistake I see isn't waiting too long — it's assuming a DJ booked for December 15th can't also handle a request-heavy office party on the 18th and a full Lebanese wedding on the 20th. A DJ who's actually built for this season reads each of those rooms completely differently. Booking early doesn't mean locking in a generic set. It means giving your DJ enough runway to actually plan the night properly, instead of showing up and guessing.

The other mistake is underestimating how fast Faqra and Mar Mikhael specifically move in this window. Both areas have a small, tight pool of venues and a huge concentration of events on the same handful of nights. If your date matters to you, October is already late — not early.
