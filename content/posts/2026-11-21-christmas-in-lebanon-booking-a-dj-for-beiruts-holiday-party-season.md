By the third week of November, my phone starts filling up with the same question: "Are you free the week of Christmas?" In Lebanon, that's not a small ask. Christmas here isn't one night. It's a season that runs from the first lit-up balcony in Achrafieh through New Year's Eve and often into Orthodox Christmas in early January. If you're planning anything during that stretch — a corporate party, a family gathering, a club night in Mar Mikhael — now is the week you lock it in.

## Why Lebanon Takes Christmas So Seriously

Lebanon has one of the largest Christian populations in the Middle East, and the country treats Christmas as a full cultural event, not just a religious one. Beirut's streets light up early. Mar Mikhael's bars compete over who has the best decorations. Faqra and the mountain towns start filling with families coming up for the ski season and the holiday itself overlaps directly with that first snow. It's a rare moment when the coast and the mountains are both busy at the same time, which is part of why December is so tightly booked for anyone doing events professionally.

**The demand isn't just Lebanese families either.** This is peak homecoming season. Diaspora Lebanese fly in from Europe, the Gulf, and further afield specifically for this window, and they want the party to match the trip. That raises the bar for the music — people flying in from Dubai or Paris for two weeks expect the set to feel like it was worth the flight.

## What Actually Changes in a Christmas Set

A good Christmas party in Beirut is not a wedding and it's not a regular club night. It sits somewhere in between, and that changes how I build the music.

- **The room is mixed-generation.** Grandparents, parents, and kids home from university are often in the same space. The set has to move between eras without losing anyone — a bit of Fairouz or golden-age Arabic music early, then a shift into disco and 90s classics as the night opens up, then current Arabic and Western hits once the floor is warm.
- **There's an emotional undertone.** For a lot of families, this is the one night everyone is actually in the same country at the same time. I pay attention to that. It affects when I bring in sentimental tracks versus pure dance-floor energy.
- **Corporate parties want polish, not chaos.** A company holiday party in Beirut or Downtown needs a clean, professional flow — welcome music, speeches, award moments, then a controlled build into the dance portion. It's closer in structure to a wedding reception than to a club night.
- **Club nights in Mar Mikhael lean harder into the party.** The bars there go all-in on decorations and atmosphere, and the crowd skipping between venues expects high energy from the first track. Open-format sets that can pivot fast between genres do best here.

## Building a Holiday Crate in Advance

I don't build a Christmas set from scratch every year. I keep a dedicated folder of holiday-adjacent tracks — festive edits, feel-good disco, Arabic classics that work for this specific mood — and refresh it each November with whatever's new. Having clean, well-tagged, DJ-ready files matters more in this period than any other time of year, because you're often moving between three or four bookings a week and there's no time to hunt for a track mid-set. ## Booking Timeline: What to Lock In Now

If you're planning something for the Christmas-to-New-Year window in Lebanon, here's the realistic timeline:

1. **Late November** — confirm your date and DJ. This is the last comfortable window before calendars fill for real.
2. **Early December** — finalize the run-of-show: arrival music, dinner or speech segments, the moment the dance floor opens.
3. **Mid-December** — confirm sound and lighting with the venue, especially if it's an outdoor terrace in Faqra or a rooftop in Beirut where temperatures drop fast after sunset.
4. **One week out** — send through any specific requests, family traditions, or songs tied to the occasion so they can be worked into the set properly rather than bolted on last minute.

### What Makes Faqra and Mar Mikhael Different in December

A mountain party in Faqra in December is a different animal from a bar night in Mar Mikhael. Faqra crowds are often multi-generational families staying for the weekend, arriving relaxed, drinking slower, and wanting warmth — both literal and musical. Mar Mikhael in December is faster, younger, and venue-hopping. **The same DJ needs a genuinely different plan for each**, not the same playlist with the volume turned up. That's the part people underestimate when they book entertainment for this season — it's not one Christmas set, it's several, depending on where and who the room is.

## What I'd Tell Anyone Booking This Season

Book early. Be specific about your crowd — ages, whether it's family or strictly adults, whether there's a corporate element with speeches. Tell your DJ about any traditions that matter to you, whether that's a particular Fairouz track at a certain moment or a request list from relatives flying in. The best December sets I've played in Lebanon worked because the host gave me that context weeks in advance, not the night of.
