Faqra changes personality with the seasons. In summer it's terraces, weddings under open sky, and long golden evenings. Once the temperature drops and the first snow settles on the ridges above Kfardebian, the whole rhythm of the place shifts. The parties move indoors, closer to fireplaces, and the crowd gets smaller, warmer, and more intentional. That shift changes how I build a set — and it's a season **Beirut families and Gulf visitors alike come to Faqra for**.

If you're planning a chalet gathering, a corporate ski weekend, or a New Year's celebration in the mountains this year, here's what actually goes into music for that world.

## Why Faqra's Winter Calendar Fills Up Fast

Ski season in Lebanon is short and unpredictable — snow cover depends on the year, and everyone books around the same handful of good weekends. That compresses demand into a tight window. Chalets get reserved early, private ski instructors get booked out, and the same is true for entertainment. If you want a **wedding DJ in Faqra** or a private party DJ for a chalet weekend during peak snow weeks, the smart move is locking it in well before the first flurries hit, not the week you check the forecast.

The Gulf connection matters here too. A large share of Faqra's winter visitors come from Doha, Dubai, and Riyadh — families who spend summer in the Gulf heat and use the Lebanese mountains as their winter escape. That means the crowd on any given night can be genuinely mixed: Lebanese regulars, Gulf visitors, European skiers passing through. A DJ working Faqra in January has to read that mix as fluidly as one working a diaspora wedding.

## What Actually Changes in a Winter Set

Summer Faqra is open-air, high-energy, built around long uninterrupted stretches on a terrace. Winter Faqra is indoor, lower ceilings, closer quarters, and the crowd tends to arrive later and stay longer once they're warm and seated. A few practical differences I plan around:

- **Volume and dynamics shift.** Indoor rooms with wood, stone, and low ceilings carry sound very differently than an open terrace. What worked outdoors at a certain volume will feel aggressive indoors. I set levels for the room, not out of habit.
- **The tempo curve starts slower.** People are settling in with mulled wine or a first round of drinks by a fire before anyone wants to move. The build to the dance floor takes longer and needs patience.
- **Chalet parties often blend generations.** Ski trips bring grandparents, teenagers, and everyone in between under one roof. Programming for that means threading 70s and 80s classics next to current Arabic and Western hits, not picking one lane.
- **Gear has to handle the cold and the space.** Mountain venues aren't always built for a full club rig. Compact CDJ setups, careful cable management for tighter rooms, and backup gear for venues where power isn't always consistent all matter more here than in a purpose-built club.

## Corporate Ski Days and Private Chalet Parties

Faqra's winter isn't just weddings and family gatherings — it's a genuine corporate season too. Companies with Beirut or Gulf offices use the mountain as a retreat: a day on the slopes followed by an evening event back at the chalet or lodge. For those, the brief is usually different from a wedding. Clients want background energy through dinner that builds into something livable by dessert, without ever tipping into a full nightclub. That's a different skill from reading a wedding floor — it's closer to the lounge programming I'd use on a Doha rooftop, just with a fireplace instead of a skyline.

For private chalet parties, the group is smaller and the room is intimate, which actually raises the bar. There's nowhere to hide a bad transition when twenty people are a few meters from the booth. Every mix has to earn its place.

## Building the Right Library for the Season

Winter sets lean on a different part of my catalog than summer club nights. More warm, mid-tempo material — soulful house, classic disco, Arabic ballads reworked into something danceable, 90s R&B — and less peak-time techno. Keeping that library organized by mood and BPM rather than just genre makes it possible to move fluidly through a long, slow-building evening instead of forcing energy that isn't there yet. ## Questions Worth Asking Before You Book a Faqra Winter DJ

If you're organizing a chalet party or a mountain corporate event this season, a few questions will tell you a lot about whether a DJ is right for the room:

1. Have you played indoor mountain venues before, and how do you adjust for the acoustics?
2. What's your backup plan if power at the venue is inconsistent?
3. Can you build a slower, seated-dinner-to-dance-floor arc, not just a peak-time club set?
4. Do you carry a system compact enough for a chalet living room or lodge, not just a club stage?
5. Can you blend Arabic, Western, and generational favorites for a mixed family or corporate crowd?

Those answers matter more in Faqra's winter season than almost anywhere else I play, precisely because the rooms are smaller and the crowd is closer.
