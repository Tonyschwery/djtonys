Every June, everyone assumes summer is Lebanon's wedding season. Rooftop terraces in Beirut, beach clubs in Batroun, the diaspora flying home for July and August. That part is true. But if you've spent 25 years behind the decks in this country like I have, you know the real secret: **September through November is just as busy — often busier — and far less predicted.**

Couples who plan around this window get better venues, better light, and better weather. Here's why autumn deserves the same attention as high summer when you're booking entertainment in Beirut or Faqra.

## Why Autumn Works Better Than Summer for Lebanese Weddings

Summer in Beirut means humidity, heat, and every venue booked solid. Autumn changes the equation:

- **Coastal temperatures ease off.** Late September and October in Beirut and along the coast still allow outdoor terraces and gardens, but without the peak-summer heat that pushes guests indoors by 11pm.
- **Faqra becomes genuinely comfortable.** The mountain venues that feel perfect in July can turn cold and windy by December. Early autumn is often the sweet spot — cool evenings, clear skies, no need for heaters yet.
- **The diaspora comes home twice.** Many Lebanese families abroad plan two trips a year: summer for the extended family gathering, and a shorter autumn or year-end trip that often lines up with a wedding, engagement, or reunion event.
- **Venues have more flexibility.** Because everyone defaults to booking June through August, September and October calendars at top venues in Beirut and the mountains actually have more room to negotiate — on both venue and vendor pricing.

If you've been holding off on locking in a date because you assumed "wedding season" means summer only, autumn is worth a second look.

## What Changes in the Music for an Autumn Wedding

Programming a set for October in Faqra is not the same as programming one for a July night in Beirut, and it's not just about temperature.

**The energy curve shifts earlier.** Cooler evenings mean guests are often ready to dance sooner — there's less of that heat-induced lull where people just want to sit with a drink. I tend to move into a faster build during the reception itself rather than waiting for a late-night second wind.

**The guest mix changes too.** Autumn weddings in Lebanon often skew slightly older and more family-heavy than the peak-summer, diaspora-reunion crowds. That means more attention to the classics — a strong run through 70s, 80s, and 90s Arabic and Western tracks that everyone in the room, from grandparents to cousins, actually knows. This is genuinely where I spend most of my prep time; it's the deepest part of my library and the part that reads a room fastest.

**Outdoor-to-indoor transitions matter more.** A lot of autumn events in Faqra and the mountains plan for an outdoor cocktail hour and reception, then move the dancing indoors once it cools down after sunset. That transition needs to be planned into the music, not just the seating — you don't want the energy to reset when everyone changes rooms.

## Booking Timeline: What to Lock In and When

For an autumn wedding in Beirut or Faqra, here's roughly how I'd advise sequencing your bookings:

1. **Venue first, always.** Autumn dates at the best mountain and coastal venues fill up faster than people expect, precisely because fewer couples are thinking about this window yet.
2. **DJ and sound within the same window as the venue.** Once you have a date and a venue, lock entertainment early — especially if the venue is outdoor-capable and you want the flexibility to move the dance floor as the temperature drops.
3. **Confirm power and sound logistics with the venue directly.** Mountain venues, especially, vary a lot in what's already wired in versus what a DJ needs to bring. This is worth confirming months out, not the week before.
4. **Finalize the music brief 4-6 weeks out.** Family names for shout-outs, the zaffe entrance, first dance, any cultural specifics for a mixed Arabic/Western guest list — all of it is easier to plan properly when it's not rushed.
5. **Do a final walk-through of run-of-show timing** with your planner and DJ together, especially for the outdoor-to-indoor handoff if the venue has one.

## A Note on Mar Mikhael and Beirut's Bar Scene in Autumn

It's not just weddings. Autumn is also when Beirut's bar and club circuit — Mar Mikhael especially — starts filling back up after the quieter, heat-drained stretch of August. Private parties, corporate nights, and engagement celebrations tend to cluster into September and October before the December calendar gets swallowed by holiday bookings. If you're planning a private party or corporate event in Beirut, this is a genuinely good window to move on — venues and entertainment both have more room to work with you on timing and format.

## The Bottom Line

Lebanon doesn't really have one wedding season. It has two: the loud, crowded summer everyone plans around, and the quieter, arguably better autumn stretch that smart couples and planners are already using. Better weather, more venue flexibility, and a guest mix that rewards a DJ who can move confidently between eras and languages in the same set.
