Formula 1 closes its season in the Gulf every year, and Qatar's race weekend at Lusail International Circuit has become one of the busiest stretches on Doha's event calendar. Hotels fill up, hospitality suites get built out, and private villas around The Pearl and Lusail Marina turn into their own mini-festivals once the cars go quiet for the night. We sat down with Tony Schwery, who plays across Doha during Grand Prix week most years, to talk about what actually happens behind the scenes.

## Why does an F1 weekend matter so much for DJ bookings in Doha?

"It compresses everything into three or four days," Tony says. "Normally a wedding season or a festival builds up over months. Grand Prix weekend arrives with teams, sponsors, travelling fans, and corporate guests all landing at once, and every one of them wants somewhere good to go after the checkered flag. You get hospitality suites at the track during the day, then hotel takeovers, rooftop parties, and private villa events at night. **Every slot gets booked fast, and the good ones go first.**"

### Isn't this the same as Bahrain or Abu Dhabi race weekends?

"The shape is similar — race by day, parties by night — but Lusail has its own character. It's a newer circuit, it's tied closely to The Pearl and Lusail Marina's nightlife, and a lot of the crowd is mixing with Doha's existing scene rather than a separate 'race town.' You're not just playing for motorsport fans. You're playing for hotel guests, corporate clients, and local residents all in the same room, sometimes the same set."

## What kind of set does a Grand Prix weekend actually need?

Tony breaks it down into a few distinct rooms he's typically asked to cover:

1. **Hospitality suites and corporate lounges** — background-to-mid energy during the day, open format, nothing that competes with conversation or sponsor speeches.
2. **Hotel rooftop and pool parties** — this is where the energy climbs. International house and open-format sets that can move from a European tech house crowd to Arabic vocals to 90s classics without losing anyone.
3. **Private villa after-parties** — later, louder, and usually the most flexible brief. "Someone always asks for a throwback set at 2am. I'll drop 80s and 90s classics because that's what actually gets a mixed international crowd singing."
4. **Club nights timed around the race** — venues programming their own takeover nights to catch the overflow crowd looking for somewhere to land after dinner.

"Each of those rooms needs a different pace," Tony says. "The mistake is treating Grand Prix weekend like one long party. It's four different jobs that happen to fall in the same four days."

## Does the international crowd change how you build a set?

"Completely. You've got Formula 1's genuinely global fan base — Europeans, Americans, Southeast Asians, plus the full mix of people who live and work in Doha — all in the same room. **You can't lean on one region's playlist.** I'm reading the floor constantly: house and tech house holds the European contingent, a well-placed Arabic or Khaleeji record brings the local crowd forward, and a run of 90s and early 2000s classics usually works for everyone at once because people know the words regardless of where they're from."

## What should someone hosting a race-weekend event actually plan for?

Tony's practical checklist for anyone booking entertainment around Grand Prix weekend in Doha:

- **Book months ahead, not weeks.** The best DJs, sound companies, and venues lock in their race-weekend calendar early. If you're planning a villa party or a brand activation, treat it like wedding season — the closer you get, the fewer good options are left.
- **Confirm sound limits before you confirm the guest list.** Villas, marina-front terraces, and hotel rooftops often sit close to neighbors or other bookings happening the same night. A rider that works for a quiet lounge set won't work for a 1am peak-time set, and vice versa.
- **Decide your crowd mix upfront.** Tell your DJ who's actually coming — team guests, sponsors, friends, a mixed local-and-international crowd — so the set can be built around the room instead of guessed on the night.
- **Separate your daytime and nighttime briefs.** A hospitality suite set and an after-party set are different jobs. Say so when you book, rather than leaving it open and hoping it works out.
- **Build in a buffer for race-day delays.** Qualifying and race schedules can run long or shift due to weather and track sessions. A flexible start time, confirmed with your DJ in advance, avoids a dead room waiting for guests who are still stuck in traffic from the circuit.

## Does Grand Prix season spill over into the rest of Doha's calendar?

"It does," Tony says. "Once people see how well a race weekend can run with the right entertainment, a lot of those bookings turn into year-round relationships — corporate parties, private celebrations, hotel residencies. Grand Prix weekend is often the first time a client sees what a full night actually looks like when the music is built for the room instead of just played at it."
