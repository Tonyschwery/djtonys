Drive twenty minutes north out of Beirut along the coastal highway and the city changes shape. The apartment towers thin out, the bay opens up, and Jounieh appears — casino lights on one side, the Harissa cable car climbing the hill on the other, waterfront terraces strung along the water all the way to Kaslik. It's one of Lebanon's oldest entertainment towns, and it's still one of its busiest.

I get asked to play here often, sometimes for a wedding in a seaside hall, sometimes for a private party in a hillside villa overlooking the bay, sometimes for a corporate night that ends up spilling onto a terrace with the Mediterranean right there. Jounieh isn't Beirut and it isn't Faqra. It has its own rhythm, and if you're planning an event here, that rhythm should shape how you book your DJ.

## What Makes Jounieh Different From Beirut or Faqra

Beirut nightlife is dense and vertical — Mar Mikhael's bar-to-bar crawl, Gemmayzeh's alleys, rooftop clubs stacked above the street. Faqra is mountain and altitude, weddings built around a view and a chalet. Jounieh is coastal and horizontal. Venues spread along the water, many of them open-air or semi-open, and the sea air is part of the experience whether you plan for it or not.

That geography changes three things for a DJ:

- **Sound has to travel outward, not just fill a room.** Waterfront terraces lose energy to open space the way an enclosed club doesn't. A system tuned for a Mar Mikhael bar will feel thin on a Jounieh terrace with the bay behind it.
- **Salt air and humidity are real considerations for gear.** Anyone running exposed vinyl, open-face controllers, or delicate outdoor lighting near the water needs to think about coverage and setup time, not just sound.
- **The crowd mixes generations more than a typical Beirut club night.** Jounieh's casino heritage means the audience often spans decades — older guests who remember the venue's golden era alongside younger crowds out for the newer lounges and beach clubs nearby.

## The Casino du Liban Effect

You can't talk about Jounieh nightlife without acknowledging the Casino du Liban's shadow over the whole town. It's one of the region's most storied entertainment venues, and for decades it set the tone for what a big night out in Lebanon looked like — live orchestras, cabaret, later resident DJs and international bookings. Even now, that legacy shapes expectations. Jounieh audiences, more than most, appreciate a set that respects **musical range and showmanship**, not just a loop of current club hits.

That's part of why 70s, 80s, and 90s programming works so well here. A crowd that grew up with the casino's golden years responds to a well-placed disco classic or an 80s anthem in a way a purely modern room might not. Building that range into a set isn't nostalgia for its own sake — it's reading exactly who's in front of you.

## Weddings and Private Events Along the Coast

Jounieh and the surrounding Keserwan coastline have become a real alternative to Beirut for wedding receptions, especially for families who want sea views without the mountain logistics of Faqra. A few things I always flag to couples booking here:

1. **Confirm indoor-outdoor flow early.** Many venues split the night between an indoor hall for dinner and speeches and an open terrace for dancing. The DJ needs to plan the transition, not just the music.
2. **Ask about generator backup.** Coastal venues, like most of Lebanon, can face power interruptions. A professional setup includes contingency for this so the night never actually stops.
3. **Decide on the Arabic-Western balance before the day.** Coastal weddings in this area tend to lean heavily into zaffe and dabke early, then shift into open-format Western sets later. Your DJ should know that arc going in, not improvise it.
4. **Check sound permit and curfew rules with the venue.** Waterfront properties often answer to different noise regulations than Beirut city venues, and it affects how late a set can legally run.

## An Underrated Booking Market

Jounieh doesn't get talked about as much as Beirut or Faqra in the Lebanese event conversation, but it should. It has waterfront lounges built for pre-dinner sets, hillside villas perfect for private parties, function halls that host some of Keserwan's biggest weddings, and a nightlife identity that's genuinely its own. For anyone planning an event here — whether it's a wedding, a birthday, or a corporate night by the water — the same principle applies as anywhere else: **book someone who understands the venue type, not just the genre.**

## Building the Right Set for a Coastal Room

Programming for Jounieh means keeping a deep, well-organized library on hand — disco and Italo tracks for the nostalgic stretch, current Arabic and Western hits for the peak, and enough lounge material to ease guests from dinner into dancing without a jarring gear change. Musicians and DJs who take crate preparation seriously usually keep that library curated in high-quality, DJ-ready formats rather than scrambling through inconsistent rips the night before a booking — sites like [topdjcrates.com](https://topdjcrates.com) are a useful resource if you're building or refreshing a set of WAV packs for exactly this kind of varied, cross-generational programming.

## Questions Worth Asking Before You Book

If you're planning something in Jounieh, Kaslik, or anywhere along that stretch of coast, ask your DJ:

- Have you played waterfront or open-air venues in this area before?
- What's your plan for sound coverage if the dance floor is partially outdoors?
- Can you build a set that moves comfortably between generations, not just genres?
- What's your backup plan for power or equipment issues?
- How do you handle the Arabic-to-Western transition across the night?

The answers tell you a lot more than a genre list ever will.
